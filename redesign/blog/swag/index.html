SWAG: what happened when I let every token choose its own positional scales

gating rotary position embeddings per token: best in-distribution loss, a real trade-off, and interpretable gates for ~$6 of GPU time

jul 31, 2026

I had an idea on my clipboard for about a week: in a transformer with rotary position embeddings, every token gets the same fixed bank of positional frequency channels. A comma resolving local syntax and a pronoun hunting for its antecedent three paragraphs back are handed identical positional geometry. What if each token could choose which scales matter to it?

The trick is doing that without breaking the thing that makes RoPE good. RoPE's whole value is that attention scores depend only on the distance between tokens — shift a passage left or right and nothing changes. Recent papers get flexibility by making the rotations themselves input-dependent, but then two identical relationships at different absolute positions stop behaving identically. I wanted the opposite: keep the geometry fixed, and let each token gate how much it uses each channel.

The mechanism ended up clean. Each RoPE channel's contribution to an attention score splits into a content part and a purely positional correction. SWAG (Scale-Wise Adaptive Gating) has every query token emit a sigmoid gate per channel that scales only the correction. Gates at 1: exactly standard RoPE. Gates at 0: position ignored, content kept. And the whole thing computes exactly — no approximation — as one inner product in a doubled head dimension, so it runs on normal fused attention kernels. There's a test in the repo verifying the fast form against the naive formula at 1e-10.

The experiment

Six variants, matched at ~5M parameters, trained under identical budgets on byte-level enwik8 (plus synthetic positional probes), 2–3 seeds each, evaluated at the training context and at 2× and 4× that length: no positions at all, standard RoPE, learned per-head frequencies, a reimplementation of token-conditioned frequencies (CARoPE-style), SWAG, and an ablation that gates whole channels instead of just the positional correction. Everything ran on Modal — about 3 GPU-hours total, roughly six dollars.

What happened

SWAG got the best language-modeling number. 1.479 bits/char vs 1.499 for standard RoPE, best of all six, consistent across seeds — but the margin is about the size of RoPE's own seed-to-seed noise, so I'd call it "reliably slightly better," not a breakthrough. It also degraded less than RoPE at every longer context.

But the interesting result is a trade-off, not a victory. The best extrapolator on natural text was the boring variant: just make the frequency values learnable. It crushed everyone at 4× context (2.31 vs SWAG's 3.25 vs RoPE's 3.58). So why bother with gating? Because the synthetic probes show what learned frequencies quietly give up. On a task that requires attending exactly k positions back, every variant scores 100% at the training length — but at 4× length, learned frequencies collapse to 54–73% on two of three seeds, and token-conditioned frequencies fall as low as 50%. SWAG stays at 100% on every seed, exactly like standard RoPE, because it never touched the geometry. Moving the frequency bank helps you extrapolate prose and breaks your ruler. SWAG keeps the ruler.

The ablation earned its slot. Gating whole channels (content and position together) matched SWAG in-distribution but lost the entire long-context advantage — same parameters, same inputs, different multiplication target. Gating the positional correction specifically is the operative choice. That's the kind of result that makes me trust the mechanism more than any headline number.

And the gates learned something legible. This was the long-shot hope, and it showed up. Digits suppress the most-local channels hard (position within "1847" barely matters). Whitespace keeps nearly everything open in layer 0. By layer 3, every character class converges on one shared mid-band scale with long wavelengths pruned. Deeper layers shed positional correction overall — fastest for newlines and whitespace. Meanwhile the per-head strength parameter λ drifted up almost everywhere (~1.3): the model turns position up globally and prunes it per token. None of that was supervised.

What didn't work

My CARoPE reimplementation trained badly — 2.79 bits/char, worse than a model with no positional information at all. I'm reporting that as a fact about my approximation at this scale, not about the original paper, whose configuration differs in details I didn't replicate. SWAG also costs 1.29× RoPE's wall-clock in my unfused implementation. And on a tiny corpus (character-level Shakespeare), SWAG's in-distribution edge disappeared entirely — the gate network plausibly needs more data than 1.1M characters to learn its structure. Copy-task length generalization failed for every variant, including SWAG; positional encodings alone don't buy you that.

All of this is at ~5M parameters. Effects that appear here may vanish at scale; effects that matter at scale may be invisible here. What the setup does support is the controlled within-study comparison — same budgets, same data, same seeds.

Where it lands

Standard RoPE: robust geometry, worst prose extrapolation. Learned frequencies: best prose extrapolation, broken ruler. SWAG: best in-distribution, better-than-RoPE extrapolation, ruler intact. Nobody wins everything, and I think the honest framing is that input-dependent positional flexibility isn't free — the papers making rotations input-dependent are trading away a property whose absence only shows up on tasks that measure it directly.

Code, paper, all 78 run records, and the gate heatmaps: github.com/jamesbaker1/swag-rope. The whole thing — implementation, tests, training, analysis, paper — ran in about a day, with the GPU work costing less than lunch.

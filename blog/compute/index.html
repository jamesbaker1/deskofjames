not your compute, not your model

open weights arrived. the machines to run them did not. on the arithmetic of local inference, the rest of the stack, and a club in new york.

crypto people produced exactly one aphorism that survived contact with reality: not your keys, not your coins. it kept being true through every collapse, which is the only test an aphorism faces. i want it with the nouns swapped: not your compute, not your model.

the setup: open models won. deepseek ships frontier-adjacent weights under mit. meta ships llama under a livable license. the weights are on hugging face tonight, all of them, free. and almost nobody who downloads them can run them.

the arithmetic

deepseek-r1 is 671 billion parameters, fp8 native: 689gb of weights before the kv cache takes a byte. llama 3.1 405b in bf16 is 810gb. the largest consumer gpu nvidia sells holds 32. quantize r1 down to 4 bits and it still wants about 400gb, which is thirteen consumer cards wired together, which you cannot do, because the interconnect is the actual product and it does not come in consumer.

the honest counterexample: apple sold a mac studio with 512gb of unified memory. it ran 4-bit r1 at seventeen or so tokens per second while the context stayed short, less as it grew. the list price was $9,499. then apple pulled the 512gb option, dram shortage, and the ceiling is 256 now. the point survives the counterexample. "you can drive to alaska" is also true.

so the license says the model is yours and the memory map says it belongs to whoever owns an 8x h100 node. those start around $250,000 racked, or by the hour from the same few clouds the weights were supposed to make optional. open weight, closed socket.

weights are the binary

calling weights "open source" is calling a compiled binary open source. the source of a model is the training data, the filtering pipeline, the training code, the schedule, the post-training recipe, and the evals that decided which checkpoint shipped. of those, a typical open release ships none. you get the artifact and a license to run it, on hardware you do not have.

the exceptions are real and worth naming: allenai's olmo ships data, code, and checkpoints; pythia did it first at smaller scale. everyone else chose the phrase "open weight" with lawyers in the room, and the phrase is accurate. weight is the part they opened.

the rest of the stack

even with weights and hardware in hand, inference is its own software problem: an engine (paged kv cache, continuous batching, quantized kernels), a scheduler that decides whose job runs when the vram runs out, a way to move jobs between machines without reading them. none of that arrives with the weights either. the stack under a running model has six layers and "weights" is one:

  • training data
  • training code and recipe
  • weights
  • inference engine
  • scheduling and routing
  • power and cooling

the fight over layer three is loud because it is the only layer with a license attached. the other five get decided by default, and the default is rented. renting is fine until the landlord deprecates the model you built on, or rate-limits you mid-experiment, or logs what you send. the fix is not everyone buying an 8x h100 node. it is thirty people splitting one, at $8,300 or so a head.

the club

so: new york compute club. members rack their own hardware in the five boroughs and the club schedules jobs across all of it. jobs travel as libsodium sealed boxes; the coordinator that routes them sees ciphertext, a node id, and a self-reported wattage. it cannot read what you run. the node you pick can, today, which is why you pick the node, and why attestation is a v2 line item instead of a marketing slide. the threat model is written down and it is not flattering, which is how you can tell it is a threat model.

the software is public and unfinished: nycc-engine is a small inference engine (paged kv, continuous batching, priority scheduling, a numpy reference model you can read in an afternoon); nycc-grid is the mesh (sealed jobs, signed node traffic, an untrusted coordinator small enough to audit); newyorkcomputeclub is the site, which renders manhattan as a load histogram. the load is invented until enough nodes exist; the buildings are not. the lights above the island are 60 hudson st, 111 8th ave, and the teleport. pull requests move your application up the pile.

the cluster is currently one node and an extension cord. that is what applications are for. they are open at newyorkcomputeclub.com/about.

the rule also has a v2 queued: not your power, not your model. an h100 turns electricity into gradients at 700 watts a card, and whoever meters the electricity holds a veto. one member has a basement in red hook and strong opinions about generators. later.

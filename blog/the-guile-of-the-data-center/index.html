The Guile of the Data Center

Don't like it? Get in the back of the line.

The tradeoff is simple, our AI overlords pronounce: procure the collective effort and power once reserved only for world war, and you too shall be out of a job (and electricity). If we fail? Well, you're in luck: you're also out of a job, because our economy has become an elaborate Rube Goldberg machine of fancy financial footwork and self-dealing to fund these efforts.

Don't like the game? Well, you're a Luddite. And you're pro-cancer. Because there's a slim chance that AI manages to pull off a somersault double flip into the keyhole-sized future where AI solves cancer, but does so without killing us all.

In law school, we called these policy decisions "risk-risk tradeoffs," a terrifyingly sterile phrase conjured up by the brains-in-vats who populate think tanks. I think the reason people are so upset is that the public bears all the downside with very little upside. Who can blame them for pushing back? Maybe we should reconsider driving 100 miles an hour into this future.
